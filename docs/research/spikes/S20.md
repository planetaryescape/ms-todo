# S20: a due-date PATCH on a recurring task, and a new task's etag

Run: 2026-09-27 19:55–19:59 UTC (a Sunday; 20:55–20:59 in London), on the `livetest` instance, in the throwaway list `ms-todo-spike-g1-2055` (deleted afterwards). Found while checking the undo picker live for D-065; ms-todo's release build and `ms-todo raw`. Task IDs shortened to their last characters.

## Answer

1. **A PATCH of `dueDateTime` on a recurring task doesn't move that task.** Graph leaves the task ID with the series, moves it on (to 00:00 UTC of the next occurrence's day), and **creates a new task** (new ID, new `createdDateTime`, `notStarted`, `recurrence` still set) with the due date asked for. Seen three times: undo of a recurring completion sending the old due date back (a day earlier), and two direct PATCHes, one to an earlier day and one to a later one. Confidence: high for what happens; the rule behind it (an "occurrence moved" exception?) is unknown.
2. **A new recurring task changes on Graph within a second of its 201.** Its etag moves and its `dueDateTime` is written back in UTC (`2026-09-26T23:00Z` for the `2026-09-27T00:00 Europe/London` sent). A completion sent before the next sync saw it gets a 412, and a comparison of the raw JSON reads the date as changed on another device. Confidence: high (seen once, explained by the rows).

## Part 2: restoring without a split (2026-09-27, 20:37–20:50 UTC)

In the throwaway list `ms-todo-spike-007-2136` (deleted afterwards). Each run: a daily recurring task POSTed as ms-todo sends it (due `2026-09-27T00:00 Europe/London`, `recurrenceTimeZone` Europe/London, which Graph stores as UTC), completed (series rolled to 09-27T23:00Z), its completed copy deleted, then:

| Sequence | Result |
|---|---|
| A: one PATCH with `dueDateTime` and `recurrence` (the pre-completion values) | **Split**: the series moved on to 09-28T00:00Z, and a new task took 09-26T00:00Z |
| B: PATCH `recurrence: null`; PATCH `dueDateTime` (sent as Graph returned it, 09-26T23:00Z); PATCH `recurrence` | One task, same ID; due 09-27T00:00Z (the date sent in UTC was truncated to the 26th, and setting the recurrence moved it to the first occurrence, the 27th) |
| C: PATCH `recurrence: null` with `dueDateTime` 2026-09-27T00:00 Europe/London; PATCH `recurrence` | **One task, same ID**, due 09-27 (local), recurrence as before; completing it again rolled it and made a copy as usual |

With ms-todo's fix (D-069, sequence C), `undo OP --copy COPY` on a real recurring completion, twice: a daily task ended with one task, its ID, due 09-27 local and its recurrence; a weekly (every Sunday) task ended with one task, due exactly `2026-09-26T23:00Z` as before, and its recurrence. The three operations (delete the copy, date with recurrence cleared, recurrence) were `done`, and the cache matched Graph. Completing the restored daily task again rolled it to the 28th with a completed copy.

## What it changes

- Undoing a recurring completion (04, D-040) deleted the copy as designed, but putting the due date back left the series moved on and added an open recurring task. Fixed by sequence C (D-069); [issue 007](../../issues/007-recurring-due-date-patch-splits.md).
- Every other due-date write to a recurring task (`tasks edit --due`, `reschedule`, My Day's due date) is likely to do the same; not checked here.
- Comparing a task's fields across a 412 must read dates as dates (D-065).
