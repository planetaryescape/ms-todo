# S8: date-phrase parser evaluation

Run: 2026-09-24, offline (no Graph calls). A throwaway Rust harness ran each candidate crate over the corpus in [S8-corpus.tsv](S8-corpus.tsv) with a fixed `now` of **2026-09-24 14:00 Europe/London (a Thursday)**.

## Answer

**No crate fits. Write a small rule-table scanner in `crates/nlp` instead.** The best engine passed 53% of the graded phrases; the two the blueprint named passed 33% (clockwords) and 24–39% (interim). Confidence: high on the conclusion, medium on individual expected values (see "Caveats").

## Method

- **Corpus:** 127 graded phrases in [S8-corpus.tsv](S8-corpus.tsv): Todoist's documented examples (`todoist`), relative phrases, absolute dates, bare times, date-plus-time combinations, titles that contain date-like words but no date (`embedded`), UK/US dialect cases, and phrases whose other tokens (`#List`, `p1`, `!`, `every …`) the date scanner must leave alone. BK's own phrases are still to come (Q10 in [12](../../blueprint/12-open-questions.md#product-questions-for-bk)).
- **Expected values** follow Todoist: a bare time already past today rolls to tomorrow, a past day and month roll to next year, and "next <weekday>" means the one in next week (UK reading). Date-only values are `YYYY-MM-DD`; timed values are `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` local.
- **Pass** means the engine found the expected span (the right substring) *and* resolved it to the expected value. Finding a date in a phrase that has none is a fail.
- **Engines:** `clockwords` 0.4.0, `interim` 0.2.1 (UK and US dialects), `chrono-english` 0.2.1 (UK) and `whichtime-sys` 0.1.0 (a port of chrono-node). `interim` and `chrono-english` parse a whole string, not free text, so the harness slid a window of up to six words over the input. "Guarded" means the window skipped a lone number and any lone word that isn't an exact day or month name; "raw" means no guard. clockwords matches for a day and an adjacent time were merged, as a real adapter would.

## Why none of them fits

- **clockwords** handles relative phrases and times inside free text, and is the best at leaving titles alone (18/20 embedded). But it has **no absolute dates at all** (0/14: no `12 oct`, `27/1`, `2026-10-12`), and no bare or abbreviated weekdays (`Friday`, `wed`, `fri 17:30`). It is built for time tracking, not due dates.
- **interim and chrono-english** are whole-string parsers with no span output, so they need the windowing adapter above. They match any word on its first three letters: `Monitor` → Monday, `Satisfy` → Saturday, `Octopus card` → October, `Decorate the` → December. A lone number becomes a year (`Buy 2 apples` → year 2). They don't roll past dates forward (`27 jan` → January 2026), `next thursday` on a Thursday lands two weeks out, and chrono-english reads no times on their own (0/11).
- **whichtime-sys** scores best (53%) and handles combinations well (11/13). But it needs `chrono::Local` as its reference (the harness had to set `TZ`), leans towards the past for weekdays (`wed` → the Wednesday just gone, `next friday` → this Friday), and still takes `Sat nav`, `Mark Wednesday Addams` and `Email Friday's report` as dates.
- **Everyone failed** on `tod`, `tom`, `eod`, `+5 days`, `friday week`, `end of month`, `mid January` and `someday`, and all five read `every last friday` as last Friday. The recurrence grammar would have to mask those anyway.

## Proposal

A custom rule-table scanner in `crates/nlp`, roughly 500–700 lines: ordered passes that mask what they claim, exact word tables instead of prefix matching, a possessive guard (`Friday's`), forward rolling, and a span for every match. One scanner serves quick add, `!`, `start` and the `--due`, `--start` and `--reminder` flags. The design is in [06](../../blueprint/06-natural-language.md#date-and-time-parsing), and the decision is D-026 in [11](../../blueprint/11-decision-log.md).

## Caveats

- Some expected values are the grader's reading of Todoist, not BK's decisions. For example, `next month` is graded as the same day next month, and `tonight`, `eod`, `morning` and `evening` have assumed hours. The headline conclusion rests on hard failures (no absolute dates, prefix matching, no spans, past-leaning weekdays) and holds whatever those semantics turn out to be.
- The exact semantics are product questions for BK, listed in [12](../../blueprint/12-open-questions.md#product-questions-for-bk).
